### What I did

I joined the project in its early phase, which meant starting with the least glamorous part: building the database and the pipelines that joined sources that lived in different places and had different criteria for what counted as a customer or a sale.

On top of that we built forecasting models and dynamic pricing prototypes, and we worked on the purchases-per-visit ratio, which was the metric that moved the client's business.

### What I took away

My first contact with high-tempo consulting, and the most useful lesson was about communication, not models.

A model with a good metric that is badly presented does not get adopted. I learned to build the narrative before the model: which decision changes, by how much, and what happens if we get it wrong. In a four-month project that matters more than tuning two points of AUC.

### Why I left

Because I wanted to work on something with more technical depth and more permanence. PwC taught me how value is delivered fast; Cajamar taught me how a model that has to survive an audit is delivered. Both are needed and I chose the second as my foundation.
