### What I studied

A year covering the full arc: the statistics that hold a model up, the machine learning that builds it, the optimisation that tunes it and the data engineering that feeds it.

The most useful part was not any specific technique, but learning to evaluate. A data science master's teaches you many models; the one that truly changes how you work is the one that teaches you to distrust your own results.

### The master's thesis

A dashboard that crosses Sentinel-2 imagery with weather data from the Agencia Estatal de Meteorología (the Spanish state meteorological agency) to monitor the condition of olive groves: vegetation indices, time series and anomaly detection comparing each plot against its own history.

It was my first project where the data source was real, public, dirty and outside my control. And the first where the audience was not a committee, but someone making decisions in the field.

### Juggling both

I did the master's while working full time. I do not recommend it for your health and I do recommend it for your priorities: it forces you to tell apart what you need to understand from what you need to deliver, which is a skill used every day and one that university does not teach.
